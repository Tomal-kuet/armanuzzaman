Overview

This course develops a strong understanding of the fundamental principles of computer security and introduces the tools and mechanisms used to protect software and computing systems. The course is intended for upper-level undergraduate students and covers cryptographic foundations and tools, authentication, access control, database security, operating-system security, software security and malware, Internet security protocols and standards, intrusion detection and prevention, privacy, identity management, security management and risk assessment, and legal and ethical aspects of cybersecurity.

Resources

Required textbook:

  • William Stallings and Lawrie Brown, Computer Security: Principles and Practice, 4th edition, Pearson, 2017.

Additional resources:

  • Charles Pfleeger and Shari Pfleeger, Security in Computing.
  • William Stallings, Cryptography and Network Security, Principles and Practice.
  • Charlie Kaufman, Radia Perlman, and Mike Speciner, Network Security: Private Communication in a Public World.
  • Edward Skoudis and Tom Liston, Counter Hack Reloaded: A Step-by-Step Guide to Computer Attacks and Effective Defenses.
  • Ross Anderson, Security Engineering: A Guide to Building Dependable Distributed Systems.
